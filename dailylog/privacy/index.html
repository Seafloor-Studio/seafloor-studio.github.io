DailyLog privacy

Last updated 17 October 2026

DailyLog has no accounts, ads, analytics or crash reporting. It doesn't collect anything about you, and nothing you write in it is sent to its makers or anyone else.

It declares no Android permissions at all, and it contains no networking code: there is nothing in the app that can reach the internet.

Where your writing goes

Your log is a plain text file you create or pick yourself, wherever you keep it. The app writes to it through the access Android gives it when you choose the file — or the folder, if you let the app name files for you, writing one file a day there — and keeps that access so it can write again next time. It can reach nothing else on your phone.

What the app keeps

On your phone, and nowhere else:

  • Your shortcuts, in a small database inside the app.
  • Your settings: which file or folder you chose, where your cursor was, the file-name template, text size and appearance.

Uninstalling the app deletes both. Your log file is yours and stays where you put it.

Backing up and restoring shortcuts

Exporting shortcuts writes a JSON file where you choose; importing reads a JSON file, or a legacy CSV one, that you pick. Those files go where you send them, and the app doesn't upload them anywhere.

Android's own backup

The app leaves Android's backup enabled, so if you have backup turned on, your shortcuts and settings can be included in the backup your phone makes. That is between you, your phone and whoever provides your backup; DailyLog plays no part in it and never sees it. Your log file is backed up, or not, according to where you keep it.
